# A Mindfulness App Improves Exam Performance in University Students

## Abstract
We evaluate "CalmStudy", a smartphone mindfulness app, on final-exam performance. Students who used the app for six weeks improved their exam scores by 12.4 points on average (p = 0.03). We conclude that CalmStudy causes better exam performance and recommend that all university students adopt it.

## Methods
Participants were recruited from one introductory statistics course at a single university. Students were invited to install CalmStudy; those who chose to install it formed the app group (n = 12), and the remaining students who volunteered for the study formed the control group (n = 11). The two groups were not otherwise matched. Prior GPA was recorded: the app group averaged 3.4 and the control group 2.9; no adjustment was made. The same instructor who taught the course graded all exams and knew which students were in the app group. Eight outcomes were measured: final exam score, midterm score, homework average, attendance, quiz average, self-reported stress, self-reported sleep, and self-reported motivation. Each was compared between groups using an independent-samples t-test at alpha = 0.05.

## Results
Table 2 reports mean change in final exam score: app group +8.1 points, control group +0.9 points (difference 7.2, p = 0.04). Two of the eight outcomes reached p < 0.05: final exam score and self-reported motivation (p = 0.03). The remaining six outcomes were not significant and are reported in the supplement. No correction for multiple comparisons was applied. Effect sizes and confidence intervals are not reported.

## Discussion
The results show that CalmStudy raises exam performance by roughly 12 points and that mindfulness training should be mandatory for all university students. Limitations are minor: the sample was small but the effect was large.
